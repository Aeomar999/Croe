# Croe - Vendor Commitment Letter

>
> **This is a non-binding statement of intent, not a contract.** Croe is pre-incorporation and pre-licence; nothing here may promise a service, imply funds will be held, or create an obligation on either side. Ask the lawyer to glance at it during the §10.1 consult - it costs nothing to add to a meeting already happening.

---

## 1. What this is actually for

**The signature is the least valuable part.** A funder cannot do anything with "a vendor said they like it."

What a funder - and you - can use is a **quantified demand statement**:

> ❌ *"I think Croe is a great idea and would use it."*
>
> ✅ *"I sell about 35 phone orders a month, averaging GHS 1,400. I lose roughly 8 sales a month to buyers who won't pay first. I would pay 2% to recover them."*

The second one is evidence. It also replaces the `[verify]` assumptions that currently drive your entire profitability case - average ticket in §10.7, and the pilot targets in §5. **Collect the numbers even from vendors who say no.**

### What makes a letter credible

| Include | Why |
|---|---|
| Real business name + verifiable social handle | A funder can check the vendor exists and is trading |
| Specific monthly volume and average order value | Turns a sentiment into a market size |
| **Lost sales per month to the trust standoff** | This is the demand number. Nothing else matters as much |
| The commission rate *they* said they'd accept | Validates or kills the §7.2 pricing decision |
| One sentence in the vendor's own words | Quotable, and proves it wasn't a form they skimmed |
| Date and signature (or recorded consent) | Freshness matters - a 9-month-old letter is stale |

### Anti-patterns that will cost you credibility

- **Do not coach the numbers.** Ask open questions, write down what they say. A funder who spots inflated figures stops trusting everything else in the deck.
- **Do not count friends and family as market evidence.** Mark them separately or leave them out.
- **Do not aggregate optimistically.** If 12 vendors signed and 4 are lukewarm, say 12 signed and 8 are strong.
- **Do not collect sensitive personal data.** Business contact only - **no Ghana Card numbers, no MoMo numbers, no ID images.** You have no lawful basis to hold them yet and no reason to want them.

## 2. The Letter

> Print on Croe letterhead once branding exists. Keep it to one page. Plain English - most vendors will not know the word "escrow", so it is explained, not assumed.

---

**LETTER OF INTENT - CROE PILOT**

Date: `_______________`

**About Croe**

Croe is a payment tool for people who sell on WhatsApp, Instagram and TikTok. When a buyer pays for an order, Croe holds the money safely until the buyer confirms the goods have arrived. Then Croe pays the seller automatically.

The buyer knows they will not lose their money. The seller knows the money is real before shipping. Neither side has to trust the other first.

**What I am saying in this letter**

I sell goods online and I have experienced buyers refusing to pay before delivery, or not paying at all after delivery. I understand what Croe is trying to build.

If Croe launches and the service works as described, **I intend to use it for my sales** and I accept the standard commission of **2.0%** on completed, successfully released orders.

I understand that:
- This letter is **not a contract**. Neither I nor Croe is obliged to anything.
- Croe is not yet operating and is completing its business registration and regulatory arrangements.
- I am not paying anything or providing any money now.

## 3. WhatsApp version

> Realistically, most social vendors will not print, sign, and scan a PDF. **A completed WhatsApp exchange is worth more than an unreturned form.** Send this, save the reply, and log it the same way.

```
Hi [name] - thanks for the chat earlier.

Quick recap: Croe holds the buyer's MoMo until they confirm the
goods arrived, then pays you automatically. Buyer can't lose
their money, you don't ship on a promise.

If it works the way I described, would you use it?

If yes, could you send me these - rough numbers are fine:

1. Orders a month, roughly?
2. Average order value?
3. How many sales a month do you lose because the buyer
   won't pay first?
4. Highest commission you'd accept?

And one line in your own words about what that problem costs you.

To be clear - nothing to pay, nothing to sign, and this isn't
live yet. I'm proving there's real demand before I build the
business side. Is it okay if I show your answers (business name
included) to funders?
```

**Accepted capture methods, in descending order of strength:** signed letter → photo of a signed letter → completed form (Google Form mirroring §2) → WhatsApp text reply → voice note with a written transcript you made. **Screenshot and archive whatever you get, with the date visible.**

## 5. Practical notes

- **Target 15–20 conversations to land 10 commitments.** Some will say no; their numbers are still useful.
- **Ask the numbers before you pitch the commission rate.** Otherwise you anchor them.
- **Demo the actual app.** You have a working product - a vendor who has held it in their hand gives a much more meaningful answer than one who heard a description.
- **Re-confirm anything older than ~3 months** before putting it in front of a funder.
- **Store letters and screenshots in one place**, backed up, with dates. This is your evidence pack.
