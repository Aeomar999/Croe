The mouth is the whole argument. Too tight and the C disappears into a ring — which is Between, badly drawn. Too wide and the enclosure stops holding anything and it becomes a bare letterform. 100° keeps both readings alive at once, which is the only reason to have picked this concept.

Two facts are load-bearing. The dot sits at the exact centre of the tile and the C is nudged right around it — so the money is what the icon is centred on, and the enclosure moves to suit it. And the aperture gap is 8.75, which is 1.75 px inside a 20 px icon: above the ~1.5 px floor where a gap silts up on a mid-range Android screen. Below 24 px that margin is gone, which is what the micro variant exists to fix.

Corrected after measuring: an earlier draft of this sheet claimed a 9.75 gap and a "never less than the stroke" rule. Both were wrong — a gap that wide is unreachable without either a 69-unit mark or a dot small enough to vanish. The real constraint is the pixel floor above.

A second drawing for small sizes is standard practice, not a hedge — Apple ships different optical sizes of its own system glyphs for exactly this reason. Use the micro variant for the favicon and the notification icon only. Everything else uses the standard drawing. If you would rather ship one drawing and accept a slightly muddier favicon, that is a legitimate call — say so and I will drop it.
The collision

The mark holds a green dot. The wordmark ends in a green full stop. Put them side by side and the brand says its one idea twice, six millimetres apart — which reads as indecision, not emphasis. One of them has to give.

The rule: exactly one green dot in any Croe lockup
With the mark present, the wordmark is croe — no period, because the mark is the period. Standing alone with no mark, the wordmark is croe. with the green full stop, exactly as it already appears on onboarding. Nothing else about the logotype changes, and no existing screen needs redrawing.
