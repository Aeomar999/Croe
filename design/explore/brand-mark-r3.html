Aperture — improved
Round 3 · the © problem, a mouth that means something, and a real logotype
What's wrong

1. Its nearest neighbour is the copyright symbol. A heavy C with something dead centre is one glyph away from ©, and closer still to a G. Round 2 never tested this.

2. The 100° mouth was chosen by eye. The stroke, the dot and the opening have no relationship to one another — three numbers that happen to sit together. A mark whose proportions mean nothing looks arbitrary even when nobody can say why.

3. The wordmark is a font, not a logotype. Plus Jakarta Sans 800 with a single global tracking value, straight off the shelf. This is the weakest part of the identity and round 2 scoped it out too readily.

A mouth that
means something

The fix for both problems is the same move. Tie the opening to the dot. Make the clear gap between the two terminals exactly one dot wide, and the mark stops being three arbitrary numbers — it becomes a vessel with a precisely sized aperture. Tighter also means less C, which is what was making it read as ©.

Why one dot wide and not narrower. A gap narrower than the dot says the money can never leave, which is not what Croe does — escrow releases, that is the whole product. A gap exactly one dot wide says this is the only way out, and it is the exact size of the thing leaving. That is the sentence the mark should be saying.

Correction — tightening the mouth does not fix the © read
I expected 72.3° to break the letter enough to kill the resemblance. Looking at the monochrome row above, it does not: at 0° the mark is still plainly a C, and in one tone it is still adjacent to © and G. Tightening the mouth buys internal logic and deliberateness — real gains — but not this one.

Only rotating the mouth off the horizontal actually kills it, and that costs the letter, which is the entire reason Aperture was chosen over Between. There is no version of this that gets both. The trade is real and it is yours to make.
My read: keep the mouth on the horizontal and accept the resemblance. It is modest in practice — the icon is never encountered unlabelled, the green dot separates it everywhere except Android themed icons, and © is not a glyph anyone expects to find on a home screen. 30° is the honest alternative if you would rather the mark be unmistakable than be a C; it is the most distinctive drawing on this page. 90° is a dead end — it reads as a face.
Resting dot

A dot resting on the floor of the vessel — settled, gravity-bound — would be warmer than one floating dead centre. It is the one idea from Cradle worth stealing. It does not survive measurement.

Dropped on measurement, not on taste.
The logotype

Four letters set in a retail font with one global tracking value. Two things are actually wrong with it, and both are fixable without a font editor.

croe.
Before — one global −.042em
croe.
After — per-pair, and no trailing space
1. The r|o pair is the hole. The r's arm ends in mid-air and the o is a full round, so the default fit leaves a visible gap right in the middle of a four-letter word. It takes −.078em against the c|r pair's −.042em — nearly double — before the four letters read as one object. Uniform tracking cannot fix this by definition: it moves every pair by the same amount, and these pairs do not have the same problem.

2. Global tracking puts phantom space after the final letter. letter-spacing applies after every glyph including the last, so −.042em silently pulls the full stop — and, in a lockup, the gap to the mark — tighter than specified. The last glyph must be set to 0. This is why the round 2 lockup gap looked slightly wrong and I could not say why.

What changed
Mouth 100° → 72.3°, so the opening is now exactly one dot wide. Everything else is untouched: same stroke, same dot, same optical nudge, same aperture clearance of 8.75 — so the 1.75 px floor at 20 px still holds and nothing in the asset spec moves.

What this buys is rigor, not disguise. The mark now has a reason for its proportions and looks deliberate at large sizes where round 2 looked approximate. It is still a C, and in monochrome it is still adjacent to © — see the correction in the orientation row above.

The logotype is the larger gain of the two.
