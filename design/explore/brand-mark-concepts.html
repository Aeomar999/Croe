Brand mark — five ways to draw "held"
Concept: held between · Ground: ink · Round 1, geometry only
The brief

Croe's mark has one job: make a stranger's payment link feel safe. It is seen first at 20 px, inside a WhatsApp preview, beside a message from someone the buyer has never met — and only later at 1024 px in a store listing. So it is designed at the small end and allowed to grow.

No lock. No shield. Both are defensive symbols that announce a threat, and every screen behind this icon is written to do the opposite — reassure first, explain second. The mark should read as holding, not defending.

Two shapes, never three: one stroke form and one green dot. The dot is the money and it is the same dot as the full stop in croe. — so the symbol and the logotype share an atom instead of being two unrelated drawings.

This row is the actual test, and it is unkind on purpose. At 20 px a mark has roughly two shapes' worth of budget — anything that needs a third shape, or a gap thinner than the stroke, turns to mush. Judge the right-hand column first and the left-hand column last.

Result: Aperture and Between both survive 20 px intact — Between better than expected, because its two gaps sit at top and bottom where nothing else competes for them. Release is the only outright failure: its detached dot fuses with the ring terminal and the whole thing collapses into a loading spinner.
Monochrome
Android 13+ themed icons strip every colour and render the foreground in one tone, so the green dot disappears. Any concept that only works because the dot is a different colour fails here. This is not an edge case — it is the default icon on a large share of Ghanaian Android phones.
On light
Ink-on-canvas, for use inside the app itself — splash, screen heads, the payment-link web page. The mark must survive the inversion without needing a second drawing.

My read
It is between Aperture and Between — and they answer different questions
Aperture is the safer, more legible mark: one closed stroke plus one dot, the smallest possible shape budget, and it is unmistakable at 20 px. Its real asset is that it carries the name — for a brand nobody has heard of yet, an icon that says C is worth a great deal.

Between is the more distinctive mark and the conceptually truer one: two halves that never touch, with the money suspended between them, which is precisely what escrow is. It is rarer in fintech than a letterform and it holds at 20 px better than I expected it to.
The honest tension: you chose "held between" over "monogram C", and Aperture quietly walks part of the way back — it is the held-between idea drawn as a letter. Whether that is the best of both or a hedge is a judgement call, and it is yours. Between is the answer with no hedge in it.

Cradle is the warmest and most human of the five, but an upward bowl is a well-worn gesture (wallets, "receive", deposit) and it surrenders the name.
Shelter is Cradle inverted and reads as a roof — closer to insurance than to escrow.
Release tells the best story (held, then paid) but is the one clear failure: the detached dot fuses with the ring terminal below ~44 px, and it pushes the mark out of its own bounding box.
